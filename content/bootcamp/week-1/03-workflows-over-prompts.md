Prompt chaining is the whole idea, and it is simpler than it sounds. Instead of one conversation that does everything, you run a sequence where each step does one job and hands its output to the next.

You are not talking to an assistant. You are running a team of six, one at a time.

## The six

**Researcher.** Gathers the raw material. What is already out there, what are people saying, what are the numbers. No opinions yet, no writing.

**Strategist.** Takes the research and decides. What is the angle, who is this for, what are we not saying. This is the step people skip, and skipping it is why the writing later feels like it could be about anything.

**Storyteller.** Turns the decision into a shape. What is the order, where does the tension sit, what does the reader need to believe first.

**Writer.** Only now does anything get drafted. And by this point the draft is mostly assembly, because the thinking is done.

**Skeptic.** A fresh conversation. "Pretend you disagree with everything written here." This is the step that catches what you were too close to see.

**Editor.** Cuts. Removes the repetition, tightens the sentences, kills the parts that were only there because you liked writing them.

## Why the order matters

Each role has one job and only what it needs. The Writer is not also trying to remember the research. The Skeptic has no attachment to the draft because it was not there when it was written.

You are not getting a better answer by asking harder. You are getting a better answer because each step is a smaller question.

## Your turn

Take something you actually need to make this week. Run all six. Keep every intermediate output, because next week you will notice that four of these six steps are the same every time you do this, and that observation is the whole of lesson five.

## What you should be able to do after this

Run a six step chain on a real piece of work, and feel the difference between arguing with one conversation for an hour and moving through six that each take five minutes.
