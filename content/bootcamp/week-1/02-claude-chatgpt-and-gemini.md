People argue about which AI is best the way they argue about football clubs. It's not a useful argument. All three big assistants are good, and each one beats the others at something.

You'll use all three in this bootcamp. This lesson is about knowing which one to open first.

## Claude: writing, thinking, building

Claude is the one this bootcamp is built around, for three reasons.

It writes well. Out of the box it sounds less like a template than the others, and it takes direction on tone better, which matters a lot in week two when you teach it your voice.

It's careful with long material. Give it a long document, a messy set of notes or a whole project and ask it to reason about all of it, and it keeps track of the whole thing.

And it has Claude Code, which is how you'll build real websites and apps from week three. That's why Claude Pro is the one paid tool on this course.

## ChatGPT: the all-rounder

ChatGPT is the most widely used AI in the world, because it does a bit of everything. It's strong at images, voice conversation, quick everyday questions and research. Its free plan is generous.

It also has Codex, OpenAI's coding agent. You won't build with it here, but it's a good second opinion on code that Claude wrote.

## Gemini: inside your Google account

If your email, documents and files live in Google, Gemini can reach them. It sits inside Gmail, Docs and Drive, and it's Google's route to AI video, which you'll use in week four. Its free plan is also generous, and Google runs student offers in some countries, so check before you pay for anything.

## Pick a home base

For the next six weeks, work like this.

**Claude is your home base.** Your context, your projects and your skills live there. Most work starts there.

**ChatGPT and Gemini are your second opinions.** When something matters, paste Claude's output into one of them, with no history, and ask what's weak. In week two you'll see why a different model catches things the first one defends.

**Use the others for what they do best.** Images in ChatGPT. Anything inside your Google files in Gemini.

## Try it: one task, three tools

Pick something real you need this week. Say it's a WhatsApp message to past customers announcing a new price list. Paste the same request, with the same details, into Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini.

Then compare them side by side. Which one sounds most like a person? Which one invented something you didn't say? Which one would you actually send?

Then take the one you liked, paste it into one of the other two in a fresh chat, and ask: *"What is weak about this message? Do not rewrite it."* That's the home base and second opinion rule in practice, and it takes about ten minutes. You'll learn more about the three tools this way than from any comparison article.

All three companies ship changes every few weeks, so any ranking you read online goes out of date fast. Judge them on your own work.

## The others you'll hear about

There are more assistants than these three. You don't need to set any of them up this week, but you should know what they are when someone mentions one.

- **Perplexity** answers questions by searching the web and showing you the sources it used. It's built for research. In week two you learn to check sources with whichever tool you use, and Perplexity is one option.
- **Meta AI** is the assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook. For many people it's the first AI they ever used, because it was already on their phone. It's fine for quick questions, but it's not where you'd do serious work.
- **Microsoft Copilot** is built into Windows and Microsoft's Office apps. Companies that run on Microsoft use it, so you'll see it in job adverts.
- **DeepSeek** is a Chinese model that's free and capable. Its own privacy policy says what you type is stored in China, so never paste anything private or anything belonging to a client into it.
- **Grok** is xAI's assistant, built into X, with a free plan.

The skills you learn on this course carry across all of them. Giving a model the right context works the same everywhere.

## What you should be able to do after this

Pick the right assistant for a task without thinking about it for long, and use a second one to check the first.
