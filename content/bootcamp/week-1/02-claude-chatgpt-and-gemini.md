People argue about which AI is best the way they argue about football clubs. It is the wrong question. The three big assistants are all good, and each one is better than the others at something.

You will use all three in this bootcamp. This lesson is about knowing which one to open first.

## Claude: writing, thinking, building

Claude is the one this bootcamp is built around, for three reasons.

It writes well. Out of the box it sounds less like a template than the others, and it takes direction on tone better, which matters a lot in week two when you teach it your voice.

It is careful with long material. Give it a long document, a messy set of notes or a whole project and ask it to reason about all of it, and it holds the thread.

And it has Claude Code, which is how you will build real websites and apps from week three. That is why Claude Pro is the one paid tool on this course.

## ChatGPT: the all-rounder

ChatGPT is the most widely used AI in the world, and it earns that by doing a bit of everything. It is strong at images, voice conversation, quick everyday questions and research. Its free plan is generous.

It also has Codex, OpenAI's coding agent. You will not build with it here, but it makes an excellent second opinion on code that Claude wrote.

## Gemini: your Google life

If your email, documents and files live in Google, Gemini can reach them. It sits inside Gmail, Docs and Drive, and it is Google's route to AI video, which you will use in week four. Its free plan is also generous, and Google runs student offers in some countries, so check before you pay for anything.

## Pick a home base

Here is the working rule for the next six weeks.

**Claude is your home base.** Your context, your projects and your skills live there. Most work starts there.

**ChatGPT and Gemini are your second opinions.** When something matters, paste Claude's output into one of them, with no history, and ask what is weak. In week two you will see why a different model catches things the first one defends.

**Use the others for what they do best.** Images in ChatGPT. Anything inside your Google files in Gemini.

## Try it: one task, three tools

Pick something real you need this week. Say it is a WhatsApp message to past customers announcing a new price list. Paste the same request, with the same details, into Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini.

Then compare them side by side. Which one sounds most like a person? Which one invented something you did not say? Which one would you actually send?

Then take the one you liked, paste it into one of the other two in a fresh chat, and ask: *"What is weak about this message? Do not rewrite it."* That is the home-base-plus-second-opinion rule in about ten minutes, and you will learn more about the three tools from it than from any comparison article.

One more thing. All three companies ship changes every few weeks, so any ranking you read online is already out of date. Judge them on your own work, not on someone else's leaderboard.

## The others you will hear about

There are more assistants than these three. You do not need to set any of them up this week, but you should know what they are when someone mentions one.

- **Perplexity** answers questions by searching the web and showing you the sources it used. It is built for research. In week two you learn to check sources with whichever tool you use, and Perplexity is one option.
- **Meta AI** is the assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook. For many people it is the first AI they ever used, because it was already on their phone. Handy for quick questions, not where you do serious work.
- **Microsoft Copilot** is built into Windows and Microsoft's Office apps. Companies that run on Microsoft use it, so you will see it in job adverts.
- **DeepSeek** is a Chinese model that is free and capable. Its own privacy policy says what you type is stored in China, so never paste anything private or anything belonging to a client into it.
- **Grok** is xAI's assistant, built into X, with a free plan.

The skills you learn on this course carry across all of them. Giving a model the right context works the same everywhere.

## What you should be able to do after this

Pick the right assistant for a task without thinking about it for long, and use a second one to check the first.
