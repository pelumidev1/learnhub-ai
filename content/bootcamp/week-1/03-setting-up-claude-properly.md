Most people use Claude as a blank chat box they open, use once and forget. By the end of this lesson yours will know who you are and what you're working on, before you type a word.

## 1. Get Pro

Claude Pro costs $20 a month, or $17 a month if you pay for a year up front. It's the one paid tool on this course, because it unlocks Claude Code, which you need from week three, and it gives you far more use than the free plan.

Before you pay, compare the price on claude.ai with the price in your phone's app store. They aren't always the same, and the difference can be large.

## 2. Install the desktop app

Download it from claude.com/download, for Mac or Windows, and sign in. It's the same Claude you get in the browser, but it stays open in its own window and you'll reach for it more.

Install the phone app too. You'll read and think on your phone, and build on your laptop.

## 3. Check what Claude remembers

Claude has memory, and it's on by default. After your chats it keeps notes about you: your role, your preferences, how you like answers laid out.

Open **Settings**, then **Memory**. Read what it has stored. Delete anything wrong. It takes two minutes, and after that Claude works from facts about you instead of guesses.

If you want a chat that isn't remembered at all, use an incognito chat.

## 4. Use Projects for real work

A Project is a room for one piece of work. It has three things a normal chat does not:

- **Instructions.** How Claude should behave in this room, every time.
- **Knowledge.** Files it can read in every chat: notes, drafts, examples of your writing.
- **Its own memory.** What Claude learns inside a project stays inside it, separate from your other work.

Free accounts get five projects. Pro gets as many as you need, and handles far more files in each.

A simple rule: if you'll come back to a piece of work more than twice, give it a project.

## 5. Your first project is you

Create a project called **About me**. In the instructions, write a few lines: who you are, where you are, what you do now, and the one sentence you wrote in week zero about what you want to build or sell.

Then add three or four real pieces of your own writing to its knowledge. Emails, posts, anything you wrote yourself.

This project is part of your week one project, and you'll keep adding to it for six weeks. Whenever the work is about you, start here instead of in a blank chat.

## What you should be able to do after this

Open Claude and start work that already knows your context, instead of explaining yourself from scratch every time.
