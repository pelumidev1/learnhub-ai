You do not need code to build something that works like an agent. This lesson uses two free tools you can share with other people today.

## Google Opal: describe it, get an app

Opal is a free Google Labs tool. You describe a small app in plain words, and it builds it as a chain of steps you can see and edit: an input, some AI steps, an output. Then you share it with a link, like a Google Doc.

Since early 2026 Opal also has an **Agent step**: instead of you fixing every step, it plans the approach, picks the tools it needs and works through them. That is the difference between a recipe and an agent from lesson one, inside one box.

Opal is available in more than 160 countries. Sign in with your Google account. If it does not open where you are, use Claude Projects below for this lesson.

## Amaka's order helper

Amaka's orders arrive as messy WhatsApp messages: *"hi pls I need red velvet for sat, my daughter turning 7, can u write happy birthday tolu, deliver lekki phase 1"*. She retypes each one into her order form.

In Opal she describes:

> *"An app where I paste a customer's WhatsApp message. It pulls out the cake, size, date, the words for the cake, and the delivery area. It checks the date against today and warns me if it is less than 24 hours away. Then it writes a short, warm confirmation message I can send back."*

Opal builds the steps. She tests it with five real messages, fixes one step that kept guessing the size, and shares the link with her sister, who helps on weekends.

Build the same shape for your own work: **messy input in, clean structured output, one check, one draft for you to send.** Notice that the app drafts. Amaka still presses send.

## Claude Projects as agents

In week one you set up a Claude Project about you. A Project becomes much more like an agent when you give it three things:

1. **Instructions that describe a job, not a personality.** Not "you are a helpful assistant", but "you turn my order messages into a delivery plan for the day, grouped by area, with anything urgent first."
2. **Knowledge it needs for that job.** Your price list, your delivery areas, your usual timings.
3. **Tools, through connectors.** Next lesson connects it to things like your Google Drive or calendar, so it can look things up instead of waiting for you to paste.

Make one Project per job. A "delivery planner" Project and a "caption writer" Project each do their job better than one Project trying to do both, the same rule as skills in week one.

## When no-code is enough

No-code agents are the right choice when:

- one person, or a small team, runs it by hand when needed
- a person checks the output before it goes anywhere
- speed of building matters more than fine control

They are the wrong choice when the agent has to run by itself on a schedule, sit inside your own website, or handle customers' data at scale. That is what the rest of this week builds.

## What you should be able to do after this

Build and share a small AI app in Opal that turns messy input into something useful, and turn a Claude Project into a focused, single-job assistant.
