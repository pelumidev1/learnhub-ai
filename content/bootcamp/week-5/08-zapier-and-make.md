You know n8n now. Open a job advert for an operations, marketing or automation role, and you'll just as often see Zapier or Make. They're the same idea with different words, and an afternoon is enough to be comfortable in either.

## Same idea, different words

| Idea | n8n | Zapier | Make |
|---|---|---|---|
| A workflow | Workflow | Zap | Scenario |
| What starts it | Trigger | Trigger | Trigger |
| Each thing it does | Node | Action | Module |
| What you pay for | Executions | Tasks | Credits |

If you can build Amaka's morning plan in n8n, you can build it in either of these.

## Zapier

The easiest of the three, with the most apps to connect to. It reads like a list: when this happens, do this, then this.

Free plan, checked on 30 September 2026: 100 tasks a month, and two-step Zaps only (one trigger, one action). The cheapest paid plan is about $20 a month billed yearly. Tasks run out quickly on busy workflows, which is why companies with heavy automation often move to Make or n8n.

## Make

A visual canvas, like n8n, and usually cheaper for workflows with many steps or branches.

Free plan, checked on 30 September 2026: 1,000 credits a month, two active scenarios, and runs no more often than every 15 minutes. The cheapest paid plan is about $9 a month.

## Which one a company will use

- **Zapier:** common in small teams and non-technical departments, because anyone can pick it up.
- **Make:** common with agencies and marketers building complex visual workflows on a budget.
- **n8n:** common with technical teams and anyone doing serious AI automation, and anyone who needs to keep data on their own servers.

On your CV, name what you have actually built with, and add the translation in an interview: *"I build in n8n; the same workflow in Zapier would be a three-step Zap."* That sentence tells an employer you understand the idea, not just one tool.

## Rebuilding one workflow in Zapier

Take the simplest version of something you built in n8n and rebuild it on Zapier's free plan. Two steps is enough:

> *When a new row appears in my Google Sheet of orders, send me an email with the order details.*

Sign up at zapier.com, create a Zap, pick Google Sheets as the trigger and Gmail as the action, test it, and turn it on. Notice what felt easier than n8n, and what you missed. That comparison, in one sentence, is a good answer to an interview question.

## What you should be able to do after this

Translate a workflow between n8n, Zapier and Make, know what each free plan allows, say which kind of company tends to use which, and build a working two-step Zap.
