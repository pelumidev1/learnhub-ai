This week you make the videos that send people to the site you built in week three. Before any tool, you write the brief. It's the week three lesson again, this time for video: the model gives you the average of everything it has seen unless you decide first.

## Why most AI video feels like nothing

Ask for "a nice ad for my cake business" and you get slow drone shots, soft music and a voice saying "indulge in something special". It looks expensive and nobody remembers it.

Videos that work were planned shot by shot before anyone pressed record, and that's what you'll do in this lesson.

## Study your references frame by frame

Find two or three short videos you wish you had made. Pick ads you stopped scrolling for, and that made you do something, rather than ones that were just nice to look at.

Then watch each one with the sound off, pausing every second or two, and write down what it actually does:

- **The first two seconds.** What's on screen, and what makes you stay?
- **Pace.** How often does something change? Count it.
- **Text.** Does it appear all at once, or word by word as it's spoken?
- **The camera.** Still, handheld, zooming in, whipping sideways between shots?
- **The person or product.** Where are they in the frame, and what are they doing?

You'll find the same thing almost every time: **a new visual beat every half-second to one second, and a new shot every two to four seconds.** Slow is what makes video feel like an advert, and people skip adverts.

Write what you find in your own words. "Text builds word by word as she says it" is useful. "It looks professional" is not.

## The hook

On Instagram and TikTok you have about two seconds. Don't open with your brand name or "Hi guys". Open with something your viewer is already thinking.

Ask Claude, in your About me project:

> *"Here is my offer: [your offer line from week two]. Write ten hooks for a 30-second vertical video, each under ten words, each one something my customer is already thinking. No questions starting with 'Are you'. No 'Did you know'."*

Pick one. Rewrite it in your own words. Amaka's: *"She forgot the cake. It's 1pm."*

## The shape of 30 seconds

Most short ads that work follow the same shape. Use it until you have a reason not to:

| Seconds | Job |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | The hook |
| 2 to 8 | The problem, shown not told |
| 8 to 20 | The product solving it |
| 20 to 26 | Proof you could defend: a real number, a real review, a real photo |
| 26 to 30 | One action, and where to do it |

Sixty seconds is the same shape with more room in the middle. Start with thirty. It's harder, and it teaches you what to cut.

## The shot list

A script says what's heard. A shot list says what is *seen*, second by second. Ask Claude:

> *"Turn this script into a shot list for a 30-second vertical video, 9:16. For each shot: the seconds it runs, what is on screen, what moves, and any text on screen. A new shot every two to four seconds. Mark which shots I can film on my phone and which need AI video or motion graphics."*

The last instruction saves you money. Some shots you can film in two minutes with your phone. Filming them is cheaper, faster and more believable than generating them.

## Your video brief

Put it all in one file, `BRIEF.md`, in a new folder for this week's videos:

```
# [Name of the video]

## The one action
[What the viewer does at the end]

## Who it is for, and what they are thinking
[One sentence]

## What my references actually do
- [Reference 1]: [what it does, frame by frame]
- [Reference 2]: [...]

## Rules
- Vertical 9:16, 30 seconds
- Colours and fonts: [the same ones as your site, from BUILD.md]
- Never: [three things, specific enough for a stranger to enforce]
- Captions on every spoken line: most people watch with the sound off

## Script
[Your hook, then the script]

## Shot list
[The table from Claude, corrected by you]
```

Every tool this week, from Flow to Remotion and HyperFrames, starts by reading this file.

## What you should be able to do after this

Break a reference video down into what it actually does, write a hook your customer is already thinking, and turn a 30-second script into a shot list and a brief that any tool can build from.
