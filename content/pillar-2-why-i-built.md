## Substack article (~700 words)

### Title: "The Platform I Couldn't Afford to Build"

**Subtitle:** Learnhub started with AWS training and a bigger vision on a shelf. Here's what changed.

**The beginning**

I started Learnhub last year with a simple conviction: technology is the most accessible ladder Africa's young people have, and most of them are standing at the bottom of it without a map.

We began where I could deliver immediately: AWS Cloud training and certification preparation. Cloud skills are in demand, certifications are legible to employers, and I could teach. People showed up. People learned.

But almost immediately, I noticed the deeper problem. Before anyone asked *"how do I pass the Cloud Practitioner exam?"*, they asked a harder question: *"is cloud even right for me?"* And behind that: *"what career should I actually pursue?"*

I was answering that question one conversation at a time. Each good answer needed the person's background, interests, finances, hours, device, bandwidth, and local job market. One-to-one advice doesn't scale. A platform could — one that assessed each person, reasoned about their fit, built them a path, and stayed available for questions.

**The wall**

I wrote that vision down. Then I priced it.

A platform like that needs engineers — several, for months. It needs design, infrastructure, iteration. We were a small training outfit, not a funded startup. The honest math said: not now. Maybe not ever.

So the vision sat in a document, and we kept teaching AWS. That's not a sad story — the training helped real people. But I knew the gap between what we did and what was needed, because I met that gap every week in someone's DMs.

**What changed**

This year, the cost of building software collapsed — not metaphorically, literally. Working with Claude Code (Anthropic's AI coding agent), one person can now do what recently required a team: architecture, database design, authentication, AI integration, deployment.

I'm not an engineer by trade. Over the past days I built, with Claude Code as my technical co-founder: a full assessment flow, an AI reasoning engine that produces ranked career matches with local salary data, a roadmap generator, progress tracking with certificates, a resource library, and a 24/7 AI coach. Secure authentication. A real database with row-level security. Deployed to production infrastructure.

The same vision from the shelf — except this time I could afford it, because the price of ambition dropped from "an engineering team" to "persistence and a subscription."

**Why this matters beyond me**

LearnHub AI exists for a specific person: the student, graduate, or career changer — usually 18 to 35, usually on a mid-tier Android phone with metered data — who is motivated but drowning in conflicting advice. The product gives them what I used to give one person at a time: a reasoned recommendation, an honest timeline, a concrete path, and someone (something — it's labeled AI, always) to ask along the way.

But there's a second story here, and it might matter more to you if you're a founder: **the list of ideas that are "too expensive to build" needs re-checking.** Mine sat on the shelf for a year past its expiry date. The constraint I planned around had quietly disappeared.

The beta opens soon, free. Next week I'll publish exactly how it was built — every phase, every prompt, including what went wrong.

**The takeaway:** revisit your shelved ideas annually. Write down *why* each one was infeasible. Constraints rot faster than visions do.
