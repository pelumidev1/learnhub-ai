The decision

Who appears on camera

This had to be settled before a word was written, because the written rule does not survive contact with video.

You appear as the person who teaches. The company still has no biography.

The rule in CLAUDE.md says LearnHub never speaks in the first person, and it scopes that to the app, the marketing pages, lesson content and platform emails. Social video is not in that list, and it could not be: nobody watches a company talk. So on these five you are on camera as the person who runs the bootcamp, saying "I" freely, the way any school shows its instructor. What still does not happen is the company narrating a personal history. The 2025 closure, the scholarships, the student who paid ₦150,000, none of that appears here. That story is yours and it belongs on your own accounts, where it is stronger anyway.

Five have no face in them

Week four of the bootcamp teaches faceless video. Publishing faceless videos is therefore not a shortcut, it is the evidence. V4 says so out loud while being the thing it describes. The rest are screen recordings, which is the honest format for teaching a tool: if you are going to claim a prompt works, show it working.

The practical gain is that five of the ten need no camera, no lighting and no shirt decision, so they can be made on any evening a talking-head day cannot be scheduled.

Production

Two production days

Day one, camera. V1, V6, V7, V8, and the front of V9.

One sitting, one shirt, one background. Film them back to back in this order: V8 first because it is twenty seconds and warms you up, then V1, V6, V7. Shoot each one twice, once straight and once faster. Vertical, phone on anything steady, window light in front of you rather than behind.

Needs first: nothing. This day can happen tonight.

Day two, screen. V2, V3, V4, V5, V10.

Screen recordings at phone aspect, or desktop recordings cropped in CapCut. Record the real thing happening. No mock-ups, no sped-up footage pretending to be real time, and if something is trimmed the cut is visible rather than hidden.

Needs first: V5 needs a real site built end to end while recording. V10 needs one agent or automation that actually runs. Neither script can be filmed before the thing exists.

Video voice

What we do not do on camera

The written rules still apply. These are the ones that only come up in video, and most of them are things that would make a school look like it is auditioning.

Always

  • The hook is a full sentence with a claim in it, spoken in the first two seconds and on screen at the same time.
  • Show the thing working. If a script claims a tool does something, the screen shows it doing it.
  • Real numbers from the real screen. Nine seconds, eleven minutes, forty runs, whatever it actually was.
  • Label a sped-up section on screen while it is sped up.
  • Burn in captions. Check the tool names survived.
  • End on the thought, not on a plea. The end card carries the link.
  • One idea per video. If it needs two, it is two videos.

Never

  • "POV:", "Watch till the end", "Part 1 of 4", or a question in the first line that you answer in the second.
  • Trending audio underneath a teaching video. It reads as a school trying to sound young.
  • Promise a job, imply a placement, or name a salary. No bootcamp can promise you a job, and saying so is stronger than the promise.
  • A countdown or a deadline that is not real. The 10 October one is real and the code enforces it, so it is the only one used.
  • Naming a competitor. "Any AI school in this country" does the work without the fight.
  • A student, a testimonial or a result that does not exist. Nobody has finished a cohort yet.
  • "Phone first" or "no laptop needed". A laptop and Claude Pro are both required and both are said out loud.
  • The 2025 closure or the scholarships. That story is yours and belongs on your own account.
  • Speeding up footage while a timer is on screen.
  • Stock footage of somebody else's office, or an AI-generated person presented as a student.
