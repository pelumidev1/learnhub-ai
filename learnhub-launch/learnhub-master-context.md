## 1. The founder story, as told

**Scope, decided 22 August: this section is for Pelumi's own marketing, and nowhere else.** It is raw material for the story video, launch posts, interviews, and anything else going out under his name and face. Keep the specifics when you use it there, because the specifics are what make people believe it.

**It does not go in the product.** Not on the masterclass page, not in the app, not in a lesson, not in an email the platform sends. Learnhub is a company, and the company does not have a personal history. A section below spells out what that means for anything writing product copy.

Pelumi came from product and digital marketing. He wanted into tech but could not find where he fit, which is a different problem from not being able to learn. He started with AWS because that is what people recommend, and a few months in realised cloud was not what he actually wanted to do.

While struggling, he noticed how many other people were stuck at the same door. That observation became Learnhub Global Academy, launched in 2025. The flagship was cloud engineering. Pricing was ₦50,000, roughly $30 at the time. Ten to fifteen students registered, and one student paid ₦150,000.

It did not survive, for two concrete reasons:

1. Cloud engineering needed every student to own a capable laptop and get a local environment running. A large share of the class could not clear that bar before lesson one.
2. There was no LMS. Building one cost money he did not have in 2025, before Claude Code and Codex existed for someone without a budget.

He paused the programme and placed his students on scholarships at another academy. He lost money and face doing it, and being willing to say so is what makes the relaunch believable.

What changed in 2026 is that AI made it possible for him to build the LMS himself, with no budget.

### The two analogies that carry the thesis

The first is Gmail and Microsoft Word. Nobody sat him down and taught him either one. He started using them and got good at them, and formal instruction never came into it.

The second is parents and mobile phones. Our parents needed walking through every step. Our generation picked the phone up and worked it out. The difference came down to exposure rather than intelligence.

Both point at the same claim. The idea that you have to become a coder first, and read a hundred pages before you are allowed to build anything, was true once and is not true now. If AI helped you write the code, the idea is still yours and the vision is still yours.

## 2. Positioning

### The three-layer market

Three groups of people use AI. Learnhub serves one of them and says so plainly, which is most of the positioning.

| Layer | Who they are | Who serves them today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build AI | AI engineers, ML engineers, researchers | University programmes, DeepLearning.AI, Hugging Face, ALX-style long programmes |
| Use AI casually | People using ChatGPT for homework, emails, curiosity | Free YouTube, OpenAI Academy, everyone |
| Use AI to build and earn | Founders, freelancers, marketers, career switchers with an idea and no technical route to it | Badly served, and that is the gap |

Learnhub is a layer three school. Copy should make that obvious quickly, because sounding like a machine learning course is the fastest way to lose these people.

### One-line positioning

> A six week AI bootcamp for people who want to build and earn with AI, not build AI. Practical projects every week, most of them from your phone, and a community that outlives the cohort.

### What we are explicitly not

- Not a machine learning or data science course
- Not a certificate mill. Students finish with something live that they built, and a certificate only if they want one
- Not theory first. Nobody reads fifty pages before their first build
- Not laptop gated. Phone first wherever the work allows it, which is the direct correction of last year's failure

## 3. The ALX teardown

The ALX AI Essentials experience is the reference point, so it is worth being precise about which parts to copy and which parts are the opening.

### What ALX gets right and we should copy

The cohort model is what makes the completion numbers work, and the gap is large. Self-paced online courses average roughly 10 to 20 percent completion, and MOOCs sit near a 12.6 percent median with some sources reporting 3 to 6 percent. Cohort-based courses report figures in the 72 to 96 percent range. Adding community elements to an existing self-paced course lifts completion 30 to 40 percent. ([Skillademia](https://www.skillademia.com/statistics/online-course-completion-statistics/), [Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/course-completion-rates))

One honest caveat worth knowing: the 90 percent-plus cohort figures trace largely to Maven's self-reported numbers, and no independent academic study verifies the head-to-head comparison. ([AI Enablement Academy](https://aienablement.academy/insights/cohort-completion-rates-honest)) Use "cohort courses finish, self-paced courses do not" as the claim. Do not quote a specific percentage in marketing.

The specific ALX mechanics that create this:

- Fixed cohort with real deadlines rather than open enrolment
- Weekly peer learning days where groups meet to share and unblock each other
- Persistent chat channels, Discord for ALX, where the learning continues past the syllabus and past the end date
- Regular check-ins from the team, so learners feel individually seen
- Deliberate difficulty, which learners consistently name as the thing that built their confidence

Pelumi's own experience confirms the durable part: the WhatsApp groups from his AI Essentials cohort are still active, people still ask questions there, and the Kenya chapters still meet monthly.

### Where ALX leaves an opening

From an independent 2026 review rather than ALX's own marketing page. Note that ALX's Trustpilot summary page contains no criticisms at all, which is what you would expect from a page a company publishes about itself, so it should not be treated as evidence.

- Attrition is heavy. In a cohort of 200 it is common for only 30 to 50 people to still be active by month six. ([McTaba](https://www.mctaba.com/learn/africa/alx-africa-review))
- Mentorship is unstructured, and it draws the most complaints. How good the peer learning gets depends entirely on who you are grouped with, and plenty of learners end up in groups where most people have dropped off and two or three carry everything.
- Job outcomes fall on the learner. No guaranteed interviews, no placement commitments, limited career coaching.
- Twelve months is a long commitment, and it filters out people who need income sooner.

### The Learnhub answer to each

| ALX weakness | Learnhub response |
| --- | --- |
| Cohorts of 200 with heavy attrition | Cap the first cohort. 30 to 40 people, so every person is known by name |
| Mentorship is a lottery | Assign small pods deliberately. Founder-led weekly calls in cohort one |
| Twelve months | Six weeks, with something shipped in every one of them |
| Job placement is the learner's problem | Week six is explicitly about packaging and selling what you built, so the target is income rather than job applications |
| Generalist output | Four specific, marketable, immediately sellable skills |
| Requires serious hardware | Phone-first by design |

## 4. Who else is in this market

### Free and funded programmes

ZeroToBuilder runs a free four week AI bootcamp in Akoka, Lagos, teaching AI tools with no coding required. AI Now Bootcamp by The Incubator NG is fully funded with centres in Warri, Port Harcourt, Abuja and Redemption City. HNG Internship is free, remote and project-based. ([ZeroToBuilder](https://zero-to-ai-builder.lovable.app/), [AI Now](https://theincubatorng.org/ai-now/index.html))

### Paid, practical, no code

SureSkill Cohort teaches AI automation with Make.com, n8n, ChatGPT and Claude, no code, over two weeks. This is the closest direct competitor in shape, and it skews toward automation rather than creation and building. ([SureSkill](https://sureskillcohort.com/))

### Established Nigerian bootcamps

AltSchool Africa, Decagon, Semicolon, TechStarters Africa. All coding-track, all longer, most considerably more expensive. ([McTaba](https://www.mctaba.com/learn/nigeria/best-coding-bootcamps-nigeria))

### What the market charges

Paid online courses in Nigeria run from roughly ₦3,500 to ₦220,000. AI course fees run from free through to seven figures for hardware-heavy robotics programmes. Note that most Nigerian AI schools hide pricing behind a contact form, which is itself an opportunity: publishing your price openly is a trust signal in this market. ([Abuja Data School](https://www.abujadataschool.com/artificial-intelligence-course-fees-your-complete-guide/), [McTaba](https://www.mctaba.com/learn/nigeria/learn-to-code-in-lagos-options))

### Where the gap is

Nobody here runs a short, paid, high-touch, phone-first cohort that ends with the student having built and packaged something they can sell. The free programmes are low touch and tied to a physical location. The paid ones are long and coding first. The automation courses skip creation and monetisation entirely.

## 5. The curriculum

> **Superseded.** The six weeks are now defined in `Learnhub-ai/content/bootcamp/CURRICULUM.md` (settled 28 September, updated 29 September to match `brochure/phone.html` and `brochure/deck.html`). This section is the August outline, kept as a record.

Six weeks, one theme per week, and one shipped deliverable every week without exception. Week six exists so the previous five convert into money.

One rule governs every module: if it cannot be demonstrated in under 20 minutes and rebuilt by the student the same day, it does not belong in this bootcamp.

### Week 0: onboarding, runs before the cohort starts

Nobody teaches anything in week 0. It exists so students do not lose day one to creating accounts.

- Accounts: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Canva, CapCut
- Community access: WhatsApp cohort group plus pod assignment
- Baseline exercise: each student states in one sentence what they want to build or sell by week six. This single sentence drives their project choices for the whole programme.

### Week 1: the AI operating skill

Prompting treated as a craft rather than a list of magic words: context, role, constraints, iteration, and knowing when to abandon a conversation and start fresh.

- Tools: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini
- **Ship:** your own bio, CV and one-line offer, rebuilt with AI and posted publicly

### Week 2: writing with AI that still sounds like you

Most AI content fails because it reads like a machine wrote it, so this week spends as much time on editing and voice as on generation.

- Voice matching, long-form structure, editing down, killing AI tells
- **Ship:** a bank of five pieces of content in your own voice, at least two published

### Week 3: content and design with AI

- Images, carousels, thumbnails, simple brand consistency
- Tools: Gemini image generation, Canva AI, Ideogram, ChatGPT images
- **Ship:** a seven day content calendar, produced and scheduled

### Week 4: video with AI

The module people want most, and the one that most justifies the price. Cover both faceless and face-forward approaches, since plenty of students will never want to be on camera.

- Scripting, generation, avatars, voice, editing, repurposing long into short
- Tools worth teaching in 2026: Google Veo 3.1 for all-round realism and native audio, Kling 3.0 for footage that reads as filmed, HeyGen for avatar and business video with strong translation and lip sync, ElevenLabs for voice, CapCut for editing, and a multi-model hub such as Higgsfield or Krea for pay-as-you-go access across Veo, Kling, Seedance and Pika without stacking subscriptions. ([HeyGen comparison](https://www.heygen.com/blog/best-ai-video-generators-tested-and-reviewed), [DIY AI](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/video-generation/best-ai-video-tools/))
- Budget note for students: Kling, Pika and Luma have entry plans around $10 a month, HeyGen starts near $24. Teach the free tiers first and be honest about where paid begins.
- **Ship:** one 30 to 60 second video, published

### Week 5: building websites and apps with AI

- Tools by student profile, based on where these products stood in 2026. Lovable suits the true non-coder who needs a working full-stack product with a database and auth inside a week. v0 has the gentlest learning curve and the cleanest output. Bolt.new runs in the browser with nothing to install locally. Claude Code is the stretch track for anyone who gets comfortable in a terminal. ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/compare/lovable-vs-bolt-vs-v0), [Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/591/ai-app-builders-comparison-2026))
- Default recommendation for this cohort: start everyone on Lovable or v0. Introduce Claude Code as the "if you want to go further" path, which also seeds interest in the advanced programme later.
- **Ship:** a live URL that works on a phone, rather than a mockup

### Week 6: turning it into money

This is the week free AI courses do not have.

- Packaging what you built into an offer, pricing it, writing the proposal, first outreach, one simple funnel
- **Ship:** one live offer plus demo day, where every student presents what they built

### Recommended external resources

No generic link dumps. Point students at the one thing that fits what they are actually trying to do. All verified in August 2026.

| Resource | Use it for | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Anthropic Academy](https://www.anthropic.com/learn) | Claude fundamentals, prompt engineering, building with Claude, agents | Free, issues certificates |
| Anthropic's interactive prompt engineering tutorial | Hands-on practice with role prompting, XML tags, chain of thought, few-shot | Free |
| [Microsoft Generative AI for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/generative-ai-for-beginners) | 21 structured lessons, prompt engineering through RAG and agents | Free, MIT licensed, actively maintained |
| Elements of AI, University of Helsinki | Conceptual grounding for students who want the "what is this actually" layer | Free |
| OpenAI Academy | ChatGPT specifically, from the source | Free |
| Google AI Essentials | The course Pelumi took. **Accuracy note:** it now sits behind Coursera's $49 per month plan with only a 7 day free trial, so it can no longer be recommended as free | Paid |

Do not send week one students to Hugging Face or DeepLearning.AI. Those are layer one resources, and pointing a layer three student at them is how you convince someone they are not clever enough to be here.

## 6. Pricing

> **Superseded 29 September 2026.** Cohort 1 is now ₦150,000 early-bird (paid in full before Saturday 10 October), ₦350,000 from Sunday 11 October, or a payment plan of ₦200,000 plus ₦150,000 with the second due before week three. 35 seats. Source: `brochure/phone.html` and `brochure/deck.html`, and `lib/bootcamp/pricing.ts` in the app. The August reasoning below is kept as a record, not as the current price.

Decided 21 August on two inputs. Delivery is live weekly calls plus recorded modules, and cohort one exists to produce proof and testimonials rather than maximum revenue. Both push the same way, toward a higher price and a smaller room.

### The numbers

| Tier | Price | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founding cohort | ₦55,000 | First 15 seats, or until 31 August midnight, whichever comes first. Pay in full only |
| Standard | ₦90,000 | From 1 September, or once the 15 founding seats are gone |
| Instalment | 2 × ₦45,000 | Standard tier only. Second payment due before week three |
| Giveaway | Free | 5 seats, stated value ₦450,000 |

Cap the cohort at 25 paid seats plus the 5 free winners, so 30 people in the room. State the cap publicly.

### Why these numbers

**₦90,000 standard.** You are selling live access to yourself for six weeks. It is the most expensive thing you own, it does not scale, and it should be priced accordingly. One student already paid ₦150,000 last year for a course with no platform behind it, so willingness well above ₦50,000 already exists inside your own audience. ₦90,000 sits under that proven ceiling and inside the ₦3,500 to ₦220,000 Nigerian market band.

**₦55,000 founding.** Barely above last year's ₦50,000, so your existing audience reads it as the old price with a real platform attached and you showing up live every week. It rewards the people who move during pre-launch without anchoring the programme at the 2025 number forever.

**25 paid seats.** A live call with 40 people is really a webinar, and people quietly stop turning up to webinars. At 30 you still know every name by week two. The cap comes out of the delivery model rather than out of marketing, and it answers the ALX problem of cohorts of 200 where most people are gone by month six.

**Instalments rather than a lower price.** Splitting the payment removes the cash barrier while the headline number and the perceived value stay intact. Discounting removes all three. Restricting instalments to the standard tier also gives the founding tier a second reason to exist beyond being cheaper.

**Not staying at ₦50,000 flat.** Holding the old price tells people nothing changed, when everything did. The bigger issue is that cohort one sets cohort two's ceiling. You can credibly raise around 1.5x per cohort once testimonials exist, so starting at ₦55,000 puts cohort two near ₦135,000 while starting at ₦50,000 caps it near ₦75,000. That gap keeps widening with every cohort you run.

**Price affects who finishes.** The competitive claim is that Learnhub cohorts finish. People who paid very little tend to disappear around week two, and people who paid nothing disappear fastest. Pricing low would quietly undermine the thing you are differentiating on. The only free seats should be the five giveaway winners, and they at least have to apply for them.

### Revenue expectation

15 founding at ₦55,000 plus 10 standard at ₦90,000 comes to ₦1,725,000 for cohort one. Treat that as the ceiling rather than the forecast. A relaunch cohort that half fills and fully finishes beats a full one that does not, because cohort two gets sold entirely on cohort one's completion stories.

### Rules

- Publish the price on the page. Most Nigerian AI schools hide theirs behind a contact form, so showing yours reads as confidence and saves you a week of DMs
- Never discount below ₦45,000. Offer instalments instead
- Never announce a price in the story video. It belongs on the page and in the launch posts
- Decide the live call day and time before you start selling, because it is the first question every serious buyer asks

### Before Wednesday, sanity check it

Message five people from your audience who fit the target and ask what they would expect to pay for six weeks of live AI training that ends with them having shipped something. Do not lead with your number. If three of the five land near ₦90,000, you are right. If all five flinch, drop standard to ₦75,000 and hold founding at ₦55,000. Leave the founding price alone either way, it is doing a different job.

### Fallback if you get cold feet

₦75,000 standard, ₦50,000 founding, cap 30. Still defensible, still above last year. Do not go below this.

### Giveaway framing

Five free seats at ₦90,000 comes to ₦450,000 given away. That number belongs in the announcement copy and in the masterclass, and nowhere near the story video.

## 7. The giveaway and the masterclass

Page copy, form questions and the full email sequence live in `learnhub-masterclass-copy.md`.

Mechanic chosen: attend the free AI masterclass, then submit a form. It produces the highest intent of the options considered, and it builds the LMS waitlist directly.

How it should run:

1. Masterclass is free, live, and recorded. Registration requires an email, which is the actual point.
2. The giveaway form goes live during the session, not before. Attendance is the filter.
3. Form asks three things: what you want to build or sell, why now, and whether you can commit six weeks. Short enough to finish on a phone.
4. Winners announced publicly on Friday 28 August, with their answers quoted. Those quotes become launch content.
5. Everyone who filled the form and did not win receives the early bird offer by email the same day. This is where most of the revenue actually comes from.

Masterclass content, which doubles as the first LMS module because it is recorded:

- Claude, and what it is genuinely better at than ChatGPT
- Claude Code, shown live, building something small end to end
- Skills, plugins, connectors and MCPs, explained without jargon
- Cowork
- ChatGPT and where it still wins
- A short honest section on what AI cannot do for you

Keep the tool demonstration to things a beginner can repeat that same evening. The temptation is to show your most impressive build, and it is worth resisting. You want people leaving the session thinking they could do that tonight, rather than thinking you are very good at this.

## 9. Brand voice

Rules for anyone or anything writing for Learnhub.

Do:

- Use the real numbers. ₦50,000, fifteen students, one person who paid ₦150,000. The specifics are what make people believe it. **In Pelumi's own marketing only**, per section 1 and section 9a
- Admit the failure plainly, **in his own marketing**. The paused cohort and the scholarships are worth more said out loud than buried. They do not appear in the product
- Speak to one person rather than to "students" or "learners"
- Name what students will be able to do, rather than what they will learn about
- Keep sentences short. Nigerian English rhythm is correct here, not something to edit out

Do not:

- No em dashes anywhere
- No "unlock," "unleash," "transform your life," "in today's fast-paced world," "the future of work"
- No guaranteed income claims and no guaranteed job claims, ever
- Keep headings in sentence case
- No stock photos of people in headsets pointing at screens
- Never quote a specific cohort completion percentage. The research behind it does not support a precise number

Tone reference: someone who went through it recently, sitting across a table from you, telling you what actually worked. Avoid the register of a school addressing an intake.

## 9a. Two voices, and which one you are writing in

Decided 22 August, after the masterclass page was written twice in the wrong one.

There are two channels and they do not share a narrator.

**Pelumi's channels.** The story video, launch posts, his socials, interviews, the live call itself. First person. His history, the failure, the scholarships, the ₦150,000 student, all of it. This is where the personal story earns its keep, and it should not be softened.

**The product.** The web app, the masterclass page, the bootcamp pages, lesson content, and every email the platform sends. Learnhub speaks. Never "I", never Pelumi's biography, never the 2025 closure. If a sentence would be false coming from a company rather than a person, it does not belong.

Two exceptions, both in the reader's voice rather than the company's, and both correct:

- Questions in an FAQ. "Do I need a laptop?" is the reader asking. Rewriting it to the third person makes an FAQ read like a manual
- Button labels. "Save my seat" is the reader speaking

What the product loses by this rule is worth naming, because it is real. "I will show you" is a promise a person makes and a company cannot, and nobody attends a company's live session. The product now has to convert on substance instead, and the personal warmth belongs on the call and in Pelumi's own posts, which is where it was always strongest.
